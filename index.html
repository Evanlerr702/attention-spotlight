Outside focus does not mean unseen. This model simplifies limited attention to the top 1–2 players. Real viewers may also understand the team through peripheral vision, commentary, and experience.

How does the model work? What should we question?

Each player’s score = 1 + preference weight × favorite indicator + performance weight × scripted performance + possession weight × possession indicator. Indicators are 1 when true and 0 otherwise. Weights are slider values ÷ 100. The top 1–2 scores become the focus. Ties use ascending shirt numbers for repeatability, with no psychological meaning.

The ball carrier, positions, and off-ball actions are scripted at each step. Changing weights does not change the attack, so you can compare the same situation. All data is hypothetical; no real viewer data was used. Off-ball contributions are counted as events, not rated by value or fed back into attention scores.

Questions to explore: Does a favorite always hold attention? Who defines performance? Could camera framing matter more than preference? Does limiting focus to two players build the outcome of overlooking teammates into the model? A next version could add camera visibility or attention duration, or invite classmates to report whom they watched in the same clip. Self-reports are not eye-tracking data.
